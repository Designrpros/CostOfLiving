How to Live on €1000/month in Europe

Updated October 2026 · first published March 2026

Whether €1,000 a month is enough depends less on you than on the country. The same money buys half as much again or more in the Balkans as at the EU average, and far less in the Nordics and Switzerland.

Where the money goes

Rent decides whether €1,000 works. In the cheapest countries, housing and utilities cost between a third and 70% less than the EU average, while food costs only a little less, and in Albania and Croatia a little more. So a budget that works in Sofia or Belgrade is mostly a cheap flat, not cheap groceries.

A sensible split is to keep rent and utilities under half of the €1,000, food around a quarter, and the rest for transport, phone and everything else. Sharing a flat is the quickest way to get rent under half.

Tips

  1. Share a flat. A room in a shared flat costs far less than a studio.
  2. Live outside the centre. One stop further out on the metro or tram often costs much less.
  3. Cook at home most days, and shop at markets and discount supermarkets.
  4. Get a monthly transport pass, or walk and cycle.
  5. Get a local SIM card where roaming costs extra. EU and EEA SIM cards roam at home prices inside the EU and EEA, but not in Switzerland, Turkey or the Balkan countries outside the EU, and SIMs from elsewhere roam at a price everywhere.
  6. Compare utility providers where you can choose your own.
