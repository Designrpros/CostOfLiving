# Finding Affordable Apartments in Europe

*Updated October 2026 · first published March 2026*

Renting in a new country means new websites, new rules and new ways to get caught out. This guide covers where to look, what the law lets a landlord ask for, and how to spot a scam.

## Deposits and agent fees: what the law says

Most countries cap the deposit by law. Ask for anything above the cap back.

| Country | Largest deposit allowed | The law |
|---------|-------------------------|---------|
| Norway | Six months' rent, paid into a deposit account in the tenant's name | [Husleieloven § 3-5](https://lovdata.no/lov/1999-03-26-17/§3-5) |
| Germany | Three months' rent without service charges, payable in three instalments | [BGB § 551](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/bgb/__551.html) |
| Italy | Three months' rent, earning interest for the tenant | [Law 392/1978, art. 11](https://www.brocardi.it/legge-equo-canone/titolo-i/capo-i/art11.html) |
| Netherlands | Two months' basic rent, for contracts from 1 July 2023 | [Rijksoverheid](https://www.rijksoverheid.nl/documenten/vraag-en-antwoord/woning-huren/woning-huren-waarborgsom) |
| France | One month's rent unfurnished, two furnished | [Service-Public.fr](https://www.service-public.gouv.fr/particuliers/vosdroits/F31269?lang=en) |
| Spain | One month's rent, plus at most two more as extra guarantee on most leases | [Ley de Arrendamientos Urbanos, art. 36](https://www.boe.es/buscar/act.php?id=BOE-A-1994-26003) |

In Norway, a deposit paid straight into the landlord's own account is not a deposit account; insist on the proper kind, which banks set up for both of you.

**Agent fees.** In Germany, the Netherlands and Spain, the tenant does not pay the agent the landlord hired. In Germany an agent may charge you only if you hired them yourself ([WoVermittG § 2](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/wovermrg/__2.html)); in Spain, agency and contract costs cannot be passed on to the tenant at all ([LAU, art. 20](https://www.boe.es/buscar/act.php?id=BOE-A-1994-26003)). Elsewhere, ask before you view who pays the agent and how much.

**Utilities** are usually on top of the rent: electricity, heating, water, internet, and sometimes building charges. Ask what is included before comparing two flats.

## Documents to have ready

1. **Passport** or national ID card
2. **Proof of income:** an employment contract or recent payslips
3. **Bank statements** showing you can pay
4. **References** from a previous landlord
5. A **credit check**, in some countries

From outside the EU and EEA you will also need your residence permit or visa, and some landlords ask for a larger deposit, within the legal cap. Keep everything as PDFs, ready to send the day you see a flat you like.

## Tips

**Before you arrive**
1. Compare rents on the city's main site for a week, so you know a fair price.
2. Join local Facebook groups for rentals and newcomers.
3. Set up alerts on more than one site; good flats go in a day.

**Negotiating**
- Rent is sometimes negotiable, especially for a longer lease.
- Moving in winter, outside the student rush in August and September, gives you more choice.

**Red flags**
- Asked to pay before you have seen the flat or the landlord
- No written contract
- The owner cannot show ID or proof they own or may let the flat
- A price far below everything else in the area
- A deposit paid to the landlord's own account where the law says otherwise

Finding the right flat takes time. Be patient, and do not rush into anything that feels wrong.
